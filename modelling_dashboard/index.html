National & Basin Stream Water Quality & Ecological Modelling

This dashboard is a showcase of 16 reproducible river-network modelling case studies built on Environment Agency River Surveillance Network (RSN) and Small Streams Network (SSN) monitoring data (plus WaterBlitz citizen science) across England's Ordnance Survey Open Rivers Network (ORN v2, 90,876 reaches). It is based on the spatial_stream_model repository, which pairs a pure-stdlib Python GIS preprocessor with an OpenMP C++ and R spatial stream modelling engine. Explore single-target water quality and ecology (CS1–CS4), multivariate & citizen-science data fusion (CS5–CS7), temporal trends & threshold exceedance risk (CS8–CS9), network audits & active-learning design (CS10–CS12), and a North West regional deep dive (CS13–CS16).

What is Scalable Spatial Stream Modelling?

  • Hydrological Connectivity: Unlike standard 2D mapping that only uses straight-line distance, stream-network geostatistics models water flowing through branching river trees—combining flow-connected Tail-Up (confluence-weighted), Tail-Down (along-channel), and overland Euclidean spatial covariance.
  • Linear O(n) Scaling (S3N + NNGP): Classical SSN models scale cubically (O(n³)) and stall on large catchments. Embedding stream kernels inside a Nearest-Neighbour Gaussian Process (NNGP) with an OpenMP C++ Euler-tour solver reduces national-scale fitting and kriging across 90,000+ reaches to seconds.
  • Beyond Univariate Kriging: Extends S3N (Kunke et al., 2025) and BRISC (Saha & Datta, 2018) with non-linear GAM/ML trends, detection-limit censoring, multivariate Factor-LMC co-kriging, multi-source reliability weighting, and Schur-complement monitoring design.

2. Plain-English Guide to Stream-Network Kriging

Linked to ORN v2

What is Kriging? Kriging is a geostatistical interpolation method that predicts values at unmonitored locations by taking a weighted average of nearby observations, where the weights come from how strongly locations correlate with one another. Unlike simple distance-based smoothing, universal regression kriging first accounts for landscape covariates (land use, soil, elevation) and then models the remaining spatial pattern—giving every unmonitored reach on ORN v2 both a best-estimate prediction and a statistically rigorous uncertainty (Standard Error, SE).

Step 1: Landscape Trend (Xβ or GAM) Baseline

First, the model predicts each reach's baseline water quality from its catchment characteristics (arable farming, urban cover, soil, elevation, baseflow). What is left over at monitored RSN sites is the spatial residual: local pollution or biological patterns that land cover alone cannot explain.

Step 2: Borrowing Along the River Tree TU + TD + Euc

Standard 2D mapping only measures straight-line distance ("as the crow flies"), ignoring that water flows downhill. Stream kriging splits spatial correlation into three physical pathways across ORN v2:

  • Tail-Up (Flow-Connected + AFV): Models solutes carried downstream with river flow; only flow-connected reaches correlate, weighted at confluences by Additive Function Values (AFV).
  • Tail-Down (Channel Distance): Links reaches along the stream channel even across confluence junctions (unconnected tributaries), capturing shared valley conditions.
  • Euclidean (Overland): Captures regional proximity across watershed divides (shared geology, rainfall, or farming belts).
Step 3: Predictions + Uncertainty (SE) Every Reach

Every unmonitored ORN v2 reach receives both a best-estimate prediction and a Kriging Standard Error (SE) measuring how confident the model is given nearby gauges.

Why "Scalable" (NNGP Stream Kriging) O(n) Speed

Exact kriging across 90,876 reaches requires inverting a 90,876 × 90,876 matrix (O(n³)), which stalls on national networks. Conditioning each reach only on its m = 15 nearest stream neighbours via an OpenMP C++ Euler-tour index reduces fitting and prediction to linear O(n) time (< 1 second).

How ORN v2 Stream Covariance Connects Reaches

Visual Diagram
Reading the Diagram:
  • Tail-Up (A → C, Green): Models solutes carried downstream with river flow (e.g. Nitrate or dissolved Phosphorus). At the confluence, Tributary A's influence on Main Stem C is scaled by its Additive Function Value (AFV = 0.65 vs. 0.35 from B) to reflect dilution. Tail-Up correlation between A and B is strictly zero because water cannot flow uphill from A into B.
  • Tail-Down (A ↔ B & A ↔ C, Amber): Connects reaches along the stream channel even across confluence junctions (such as unconnected Tributaries A and B), capturing shared valley geology, riparian land use, or mobile ecology.
  • Euclidean (C ↔ D, Purple): Bridges across the catchment divide using straight-line overland distance, capturing regional climate, rainfall, or bedrock patterns shared by neighbouring basins.
  • Combined (Joint TU + TD + Euclidean): Real rivers experience all three mechanisms at once, so the model estimates the variance share of each pathway directly from the RSN observations.

3. Kriging vs. Co-Kriging (Factor-LMC)

Single-Target vs. Joint Multi-Target

Standard stream kriging models one determinand at a time. Co-Kriging extends this to multiple correlated determinands simultaneously using a Linear Model of Coregionalization (Factor-LMC), sharing Tail-Up, Tail-Down, and Euclidean latent factors across variables so predictions stay physically consistent across all 90,876 reaches.

Univariate Kriging (Single Target) CS1 – CS4
  • How it predicts: Uses only observations of the target variable itself (e.g. Nitrate 0117 alone) plus landscape covariates.
  • At unmonitored reaches: Borrows spatial information strictly from nearby gauges that also measured that same variable.
  • Best when: Monitoring coverage for the target is dense enough on its own, or no strongly correlated auxiliary variable is available.
Multivariate Co-Kriging (Factor-LMC) CS5 – CS7
  • How it predicts: Jointly models P ≥ 2 correlated targets (e.g. Nitrate + Orthophosphate, or Nutrients + Macrophytes) and their cross-covariances.
  • At unmonitored reaches: Borrows strength from both nearby gauges of the target variable and co-located auxiliary variables measured at that reach (heterotopic sampling).
  • Best when: One variable is sparser or noisier than a correlated partner—cutting prediction uncertainty at monitoring gaps.

4. Key Statistical Elements & Model Evaluation Metrics

Model Building Blocks

A. How We Measure Accuracy & Uncertainty

Validation

Cross-Validation (Random, Catchment & Upstream/Downstream)

Holds out a subset of stations (e.g., 20% or K-fold) and predicts them unseen. Catchment-blocked and upstream/downstream splits are stricter than random splits and test true generalisation to unmonitored rivers.

Wikipedia: CV ↗
Higher is better (max 1.0)

R² (In-Sample vs. CV R²)

Share of variance explained by the model: 1.0 is exact, 0 is no better than predicting the mean, and < 0 is worse than the mean. Always compare models on out-of-sample CV R², not in-sample R² (which can overfit).

Wikipedia: R² ↗
Lower is better (0 = exact)

RMSE & MAE (Prediction Error)

Average prediction error in the determinand's own units (e.g., mg/L or taxa). RMSE squares errors before averaging so it penalises large misses heavily; MAE measures typical error magnitude with less sensitivity to outliers.

Wikipedia: RMSE ↗ Wikipedia: MAE ↗
Lower SE = higher confidence

Kriging SE, Prediction Intervals & Exceedance

Every reach receives a local standard error (SE)—tight near monitoring stations and wider in unmonitored headwaters. SE drives 95% prediction intervals and 5-tier threshold exceedance probabilities (e.g., 11.3 mg/L NO3-N).

Wikipedia: SE ↗

B. Statistical Methods Inside the Models

Trend Model

Linear OLS vs. Penalized GAM Splines

Landscape covariates (elevation, slope, baseflow, soil, land cover) are fitted either as linear slopes (OLS) or as smooth non-linear curves (GAM cubic regression splines with REML smoothness penalties to prevent overfitting).

Wikipedia: OLS ↗ Wikipedia: GAM ↗
Spatial Structure

Variance Decomposition: Sill, Nugget & Range

Splits residual variance into structured spatial signal (sill: Tail-Up + Tail-Down + Euclidean) vs. unstructured noise (nugget: measurement error & micro-variation), plus range (distance over which correlation decays). Higher structured share means stronger spatial predictability.

Wikipedia: Variogram ↗
Estimation & Scaling

REML Estimation & NNGP Approximation

REML estimates spatial variance parameters without downward bias from fixed-effect degrees of freedom. NNGP conditions each reach on a small local neighbourhood so kriging scales cleanly to ~90,000+ reaches.

Wikipedia: REML ↗ Wikipedia: Gaussian Process ↗
Distributional Fit

Response Transforms & Back-Transformation

Log, Sqrt, and Box-Cox transforms keep skewed concentrations strictly positive and residuals near-Gaussian, with bias correction to avoid Jensen's inequality under-prediction when returning to native units.

Wikipedia: Box-Cox ↗ Wikipedia: Jensen's ↗
Data Handling

Censored Data (Tobit / ROS) & Observation Weights

Handles lab < LOD limits and citizen-science range brackets statistically rather than dropping or halving values, and applies inverse-variance weights so noisier sources (e.g., WaterBlitz test strips) do not distort lab RSN data.

Wikipedia: Tobit ↗ Wikipedia: Weighting ↗
Multivariate

Factor-LMC Cross-Covariance & Parameter CIs

Jointly estimates cross-correlations between determinands across Tail-Up, Tail-Down, and Nugget components so densely sampled variables improve sparse ones, paired with bootstrap/Hessian confidence intervals on model parameters.

Wikipedia: Bootstrap ↗ Wikipedia: CIs ↗

Case Study 1 · National Univariate Regression Kriging

Nitrate as N (0117): Linear S3N vs. Non-Linear GAM + S3N

Demonstrates full national (90,876 reaches) and regional basin (Humber, Thames, Severn) modelling on EA River Surveillance Network (RSN) Nitrate samples (18,462 samples across 1,210 observed reaches), comparing standard linear fixed effects against REML-penalized cubic regression splines (--trend=gam).

How Univariate Stream-Network Regression Kriging Works

  • Trend + Spatial Residuals: Models a single determinand by combining a landscape regression trend (linear covariates or non-linear GAM splines on elevation, slope, baseflow, soil, and land cover) with a Joint NNGP spatial residual surface kriged across all 90,876 reaches.
  • Tail-Up (Flow-Connected + AFV): Captures solutes transported downstream with river flow; only flow-connected reaches correlate, weighted at confluences by Additive Function Values (AFV) to reflect each tributary's upstream contribution.
  • Tail-Down & Euclidean Covariance: Tail-Down links reaches by along-channel distance even across confluence junctions (unconnected tributaries), while Euclidean captures overland proximity across catchment divides (e.g. shared geology, climate, or regional land use).
  • What "Combined" Means (Joint TU + TD + Euclidean): Instead of forcing a single spatial mechanism, S3N Combined estimates all three variance components together (σ²TU + σ²TD + σ²Euc + τ²) in one unified NNGP kriging system—automatically weighting how much variation arises from downstream flow, channel proximity, and regional overland gradients.

National Nitrate Map, Basin Zoom & Robust Z-Score

Suggested Interpretation

  • East–West Gradient: Arable Chalk/Limestone catchments in eastern & southern England consistently exceed 5–9 mg/L N, while upland Wales & Pennine headwaters remain low (< 0.5 mg/L).
  • Robust Z-Score View (--norm=robust_zscore): Standardizes predictions as (x − median) / (1.4826 × MAD) in model space, stripping concentration units so anomalous arable sub-catchments (red, > +1.5 SD) and pristine upland headwaters (blue, < −1.5 SD) stand out cleanly on one symmetric scale.

Case Study 9 · Regulatory Compliance & Uncertainty

Threshold Exceedance Risk

5-tier probabilistic threshold exceedance mapping (--threshold=uk) for Nitrate (11.3 mg/L) & Orthophosphate (0.069 mg/L), plus measurement-error / censoring workflows.

Method Explainer: Probabilistic Threshold Exceedance, 5-Tier Categories & UK Reference Values

  • What Threshold Exceedance Is: Instead of comparing only a point prediction against a limit, the model combines the predictive mean ẑ(s0) and kriging standard error SE(s0) on the transformed model scale to evaluate the exact Gaussian exceedance probability P(Y(s0) > T) = 1 − Φ((g(T) − ẑ(s0)) / SE(s0)).
  • The 5 Statistical Confidence Categories: Reaches are grouped into Confidently Compliant (< 5%), Likely Compliant (5–20%), Uncertain / Priority Monitoring (20–80%, where uncertainty makes compliance ambiguous and additional sampling is most valuable), Likely Exceeding (80–95%), and Confidently Exceeding (> 95%). These are statistical confidence bands around a single threshold T, not site-typed ecological status classes.
  • Where the Threshold Values Come From (--threshold=uk): Nitrate (11.3 mg/L as N) comes from the UK Drinking Water Inspectorate (DWI) / Nitrates Directive standard (11.3 mg/L N ≡ 50 mg/L NO3). Orthophosphate (0.069 mg/L as P) is the indicative lowland, high-alkalinity Good/Moderate SRP boundary from The Standards and Classification Directions (England and Wales) 2015 (used as a uniform national benchmark here, though site-specific regulatory boundaries vary with altitude and alkalinity).

Threshold Exceedance Risk

Suggested Interpretation

  • 5-Tier Probability Compliance Bands: Yellow (Uncertain / Priority Monitoring) marks reaches whose Gaussian exceedance probability lies between 0.20 and 0.80 around a single indicative threshold (47.5% of England reaches for Orthophosphate 0.069 mg/L)—prime candidates for follow-up sampling (separate from site-typed ecological status).
  • Nitrate vs. Phosphate Risk Profiles: Nitrate remains 90.8% confidently/likely compliant against the 11.3 mg/L N drinking-water threshold (only 0.08% exceeding), whereas Orthophosphate exhibits widespread uncertain (47.5%) and exceeding (7.3%) reaches across lowland arable and peri-urban catchments.

Case Study 8 · Multi-Period Batching & Dynamic Borrowing

Spatio-Temporal Nitrate Trends (2021–2024 & Seasonal Cycles)

Demonstrates temporal slicing via --by=year and --by=season paired with precision-weighted Dynamic Temporal Borrowing (--st-borrow), reach-level Weighted Least Squares trend tests (fig6), and consecutive-season shift surfaces (fig7).

Method Explainer: Precision-Weighted Dynamic Temporal Borrowing (--st-borrow)

  • Per-Slice Kriging First: Each year or season slice is fitted separately with Joint S3N to obtain a reach-level prediction ẑ(s, t) and kriging standard error SE(s, t).
  • Inverse-Variance Borrowing Across Time: Under --st-borrow, each slice is shrunk toward adjacent time periods using precision weights 1 / SE(s, t)² modulated by the empirical temporal correlation ρt. Reaches with high uncertainty in a sparsely sampled period borrow strength from neighboring periods, whereas well-monitored reaches with low SE retain their local signal.
  • Stabilised Trend & Transition Tests: The borrowed series feeds reach-level Weighted Least Squares slope tests (fig6) and consecutive-period transition maps (fig7), preventing isolated sampling gaps from appearing as false step-changes.

4-Panel England Temporal Grids (fig5)

Reach Trend Significance & Transitions

What Trend Direction & Significance Show: Reach slopes are estimated via precision-weighted Least Squares (w = 1 / SE²) across consecutive periods after --st-borrow. Positive slopes (red) indicate rising Nitrate concentrations over time, negative slopes (blue) indicate declining/improving trajectories, and neutral tones indicate stable reaches. Significance (Wald |Z| tiers) tests whether the fitted slope exceeds combined kriging uncertainty across periods—filtering out noisy, sparsely monitored reaches so highlighted corridors reflect sustained multi-period change rather than single-campaign noise. Season Transitions show consecutive period-to-period hydrological flushing and dilution shifts.

Suggested Interpretation

  • Winter Peak Hydrology: Notice Winter Nitrate achieves higher national CV R² (0.7164) than Summer because sustained winter runoff connects agricultural soils cleanly along flow paths.
  • Why --st-borrow Matters: When a tributary gauge is missed during one summer campaign, Dynamic Borrowing shrinks the single-period kriging variance toward neighboring periods weighted by 1 / SE², avoiding false jump artefacts in fig6.

Case Study 5 · Heterotopic Coregionalization & Network Fusion

Factor-LMC Co-Kriging (0117 + 0180) & Multi-Source Fusion (RSN + SSN)

Method Explainer: Factor-LMC Co-Kriging & RSN + SSN Fusion

  • What Factor-LMC Co-Kriging Does: Models multiple coupled determinands (here Nitrate 0117 and Orthophosphate 0180) simultaneously through a shared Linear Model of Coregionalisation across Tail-Up, Tail-Down, and Euclidean NNGP components. Case Study 10 · Environmental Gradients, Bias & Cross-Run Audits

Descriptive Statistics, Monitoring Bias & N:P Stoichiometry

Inspects monitoring bias between sampled vs. unmonitored upland reaches (05_descriptive_stats.R) and compares Nitrate vs. Orthophosphate landscapes with Fisher z and Wald Z-tests (04_compare_model_runs.R).

What is Monitoring Bias & How is it Calculated?
  • Why it occurs: Surveillance sites (e.g. RSN) are disproportionately located on larger, lowland, higher-order rivers rather than small upland headwaters, so raw station averages can overstate national nutrient levels.
  • How it is calculated: 05_descriptive_stats.R compares observed station concentrations (Obs Median) against full-network spatial predictions across all 90,876 reaches (Pred Median) via the Predicted-to-Observed Median Ratio (Pred / Obs; values < 1.0 mean stations over-sample high-concentration reaches) and a two-sample Mann-Whitney U test.
What is N:P Stoichiometry & What Does it Tell Us?
  • Definition: The atomic/molar ratio of Nitrate-N (0117) to Orthophosphate-P (0180) at each reach, computed as Molar N:P = 2.21135 × (NO3-N / PO4-P).
  • Limitation regimes: Diagnoses which nutrient limits algal and macrophyte growth: P-Limited (> 20:1; excess N, P reduction is primary lever), Co-Limited (10:1–20:1; near-Redfield balance), and N-Limited (< 10:1; low-nitrate upland headwaters or P-rich point-source corridors).

Descriptive Stats & Monitoring Bias

Reading the Representativeness Bias Table: Comparing the raw median across the 1,210 monitored RSN stations (Obs Median = 3.349 mg/L) against the spatially predicted median across all 90,876 England reaches (Pred Median = 2.059 mg/L) yields a Pred / Obs ratio of 0.615. This shows that unmonitored upland headwaters pull the true network median 38.5% below the station median, confirmed as highly significant by the Mann-Whitney U test (p < 10−44).

Suggested Interpretation

  • Statistically Significant Lowland Bias: Monitored RSN median Nitrate (3.35 mg/L) significantly exceeds the full-network predicted median (2.06 mg/L, Mann-Whitney p = 5.1 × 10−45)—proving naive sample averages overstate national nitrate pollution because headwaters are under-sampled relative to their topological abundance.
  • Landscape & N:P Coupling: Comparing Nitrate and Orthophosphate runs on the shared 90,876-reach network isolates co-elevated agricultural/urban nutrient hotspots from geological or point-source-driven Phosphate corridors via formal Fisher z and Wald Z-tests.

Case Study 11 · Station-to-Reach Snapping Geometry & Outlier QC

Station-to-Reach Snapping Audits (ORN v2)

Audits monitoring station-to-reach snapping accuracy and off-network outlier filtering across England, Thames, and Humber catchments (06_station_snapping_diagnostics.R).

How Station-to-Reach Snapping Works

  • 2 km Spatial Hash Grid: Buckets station coordinates into 2 km × 2 km cells so each of the ORN v2 reaches only tests nearby candidate stations rather than scanning the full national dataset. Each reach pads its bounding box by max(1,500 m, max_snap_m + 500 m) and queries all overlapping and adjacent grid cells, ensuring stations near bucket borders are never missed.
  • Orthogonal Segment Projection: Projects each station orthogonally onto its nearest upstream-to-downstream polyline segment (Fnode → Tnode), recording the perpendicular snapping offset (d in metres) and cumulative distance along the reach (along) to compute exact hydrologic distance to the river mouth: obs_up_dist_km = max(0, (Dist2Mth − along) / 1000).
  • Outlier Threshold Filtering (--max-snap-dist=250): Retains stations within 250 m of a reach (1,349 / 1,382 RSN stations, median 8.0 m) and excludes off-network estuarine, canal, or pond monitors so they cannot corrupt dendritic Tail-Up/Tail-Down stream distances.

Station-to-Reach Snapping Geometry & Catchment Insets

Suggested Interpretation

  • Tight Geographic Snapping: 88.6% of Humber and 83.6% of Thames stations snap within 10 m of an ORN v2 reach (2.3–3.1 m median offset).
  • Off-Network Outlier Filtering: Stations beyond the 250 m snapping threshold are automatically flagged and excluded so estuarine, canal, or pond monitors do not corrupt dendritic Tail-Up/Tail-Down hydrological distances.

Case Study 6 · Citizen Science Fusion (RSN + SSN + Earthwatch WaterBlitz)

Equal-Source vs. Surveillance-Favoured Co-Kriging (Nitrate & Orthophosphate)

Ingests 44,982 England rows from data/Waterblitz/ (36,956 citizen sites, Tobit interval-censored via format_custom_data.py) and snaps them onto ORN v2 alongside EA surveillance RSN + SSN monitors (33,013 snapped stations across 8,346 active reaches and 268 catchments). We compare Equal Sources against Favoured RSN+SSN (--prefer-source=rsn,ssn --source-weight=rsn:1,ssn:1,waterblitz:0.25) for bivariate 0117 + 0180 Co-Kriging.

Method Explainer: Equal Sources vs. Favoured RSN+SSN

  • Equal Sources (w = 1.0 for all networks): Pools RSN, SSN, and WaterBlitz observations with identical weight (1.0) and zero co-located priority. On shared reaches, high-volume citizen-science test-strip samples are averaged directly alongside EA surveillance laboratory measurements.
  • Favoured RSN+SSN (--prefer-source=rsn,ssn + --source-weight=rsn:1,ssn:1,waterblitz:0.25): Combines hard co-located priority (dropping 2,903 Nitrate and 2,672 Phosphate WaterBlitz samples on the 384 reaches that also have RSN/SSN monitors) with soft reliability weighting on WaterBlitz-only reaches. Setting waterblitz:0.25 injects an analytical variance penalty V_wt = (1/0.25 − 1)τ² = 3τ² onto citizen measurements while keeping RSN and SSN at w = 1.0 (0τ² penalty), preserving gap-filling coverage without diluting surveillance accuracy.

Spatial Predictions & Equal vs. Favoured Difference Maps

What the Difference Maps Show (Favoured RSN+SSN − Equal Sources): Maps the reach-by-reach change across all 95,773 England reaches when EA surveillance RSN+SSN observations are prioritised and WaterBlitz is weighted at 0.25. Positive shifts highlight co-located reaches and surrounding tributaries where removing citizen-science dilution lifts predictions back toward surveillance lab levels, while negative shifts show where surveillance anchors pull down isolated citizen-science spikes; surveillance-only corridors remain near zero (r > 0.99).

Suggested Interpretation

  • Co-Located Surveillance Dilution Corrected: Under Equal Sources, lower citizen-science medians (1.415 mg/L NO3-N, 0.0319 mg/L PO4-P) pulled down the 384 co-located surveillance reaches (0117 median 2.6232 → 3.4333 mg/L under Favoured, mean shift 0.6252 mg/L; 0180 median 0.0578 → 0.0645 mg/L). Dropping 2,903 (0117) and 2,672 (0180) lower-priority citizen samples on shared reaches lets EA surveillance lab measurements anchor those locations.
  • Targeted Precision Where Each Source Fits Best: Weighting WaterBlitz at 0.25 injects analytical variance penalty V_wt = (1/0.25 - 1)τ² = 3τ², inflating Mean SE by +253.58% on WaterBlitz-only citizen reaches (0.1746 → 0.6174 for 0117; +119.33% for 0180) while tightening co-located surveillance SE by -46.70% (0117) and -49.93% (0180). Across all 95,773 England reaches, mean Kriging SE drops -14.53% (0117: 0.9306 → 0.7954) and -23.43% (0180: 1.1020 → 0.8438).
  • Cross-Covariance & Threshold Priority Mapping: Because WaterBlitz kit noise is de-weighted, England latent total cross-correlation strengthens from +0.2989 (Equal) to +0.4310 (Favoured), and Uncertain Phosphate reaches (0.20 ≤ P ≤ 0.80 at 0.069 mg/L) drop from 48,802 (51.0%) to 39,969 (41.7%). Case Study 2 · Response Scale Transformations (Nitrate-N 0117)

Raw vs. Log vs. Square-Root vs. Profile-MLE Box-Cox on England Nitrate

Evaluates four response transformations on identical England RSN Nitrate observations (1,210 stations, 90,876 reaches, 267 catchments, management threshold 11.3 mg/L): untransformed --transform=none, natural log --transform=log, square root --transform=sqrt, and Profile-MLE Box-Cox --transform=boxcox (which estimates λ = 0.50 from the data). Compares model-scale CV accuracy, raw-scale prediction error, Gaussian residual symmetry, physical positivity, and 5-tier threshold stability.

National Nitrate Maps & Transform Divergence

Suggested Interpretation

  • Why Untransformed Raw (none) Breaks Physical Positivity: On native mg/L, Raw reaches R²_Raw = 0.5335 only because OLS and Kriging minimize raw squared error directly. In exchange, it produces 47 negative CV predictions and 6,349 negative England reaches (6.99% of the network, min -4.817 mg/L, P05 -0.288 mg/L) in cleaner upland catchments (Lake District, Pennines, Devon), alongside strongly right-skewed CV residuals (+1.398).
  • Why Log (ln) Tops Model-Scale R² Yet Shows Median Back-Transform Bias: Natural log leads model-scale CV (R²_Model = 0.6784, zero negatives) and sets the baseline rank ordering, but over-compresses Nitrate into left-skewed residuals (-0.904). Because exponentiating Gaussian log-predictions exp(μ) targets the conditional median rather than the arithmetic mean (Jensen’s inequality), raw-scale bias reaches -1.0078 mg/L (R²_Raw = 0.3859, RMSE_Raw = 2.7513) and wider log back-transform intervals push Uncertain reaches around 11.3 mg/L up to 9.22% (vs 1.54%–2.15% for the other transforms).
  • Why Sqrt & Profile-MLE Box-Cox (λ = 0.50) Hit the Sweet Spot: Data-driven Profile-MLE Box-Cox independently selects λ = 0.50 (2(sqrt(y)−1), affine-equivalent to sqrt), confirming square-root normalization matches English Nitrate’s mild right skew (1.001 → 0.118). Both achieve nearly Gaussian CV residuals (+0.184 / +0.185), zero negative reaches (0.00%), the lowest raw-scale MAE (1.5704 mg/L) and MedAE (0.9647 mg/L), the strongest regional CV in Thames (0.1906) and Severn (0.4031), and ρ = 0.9818 Spearman rank concordance with Log.

Case Study 12 · Active-Learning Optimal Monitoring Design (--design-sites=50)

Pinpointing Uncertainty Gaps & Highest-Impact Station Placements (Nitrate 0117)

Runs our OpenMP C++ greedy Schur-complement solver (C_s3n_optimal_design) across 90,876 England ORN v2 reaches (1,210 existing RSN stations, 267 catchments) as well as independent Humber, Thames, and Severn optimizations. Evaluates both stream-network covariance (Tail-Up + Tail-Down) and Euclidean correlation so new stations maximize network-wide uncertainty reduction without clustering next to redundant reaches.

How the C++ Greedy Schur-Complement Solver Works

  • Why not just pick the highest-SE reaches? Placing gauges solely where Kriging Standard Error (SE) is highest can waste visits on short, isolated headwater twigs or cluster multiple new sites onto the same stream.
  • What the Schur-complement calculation does: Using the fitted hybrid covariance (Tail-Up + Tail-Down + Euclidean), the Schur complement computes how much observing a single candidate reach c (with nugget variance τ²) reduces prediction variance across all connected downstream and neighbouring reaches combined.
  • Sequential greedy conditioning: In parallel via OpenMP, C_s3n_optimal_design scores all unmonitored candidate reaches (thinned to 4,000 evenly spaced reaches on national domains), selects site #1 with the largest total variance drop, and updates the remaining uncertainty map via a rank-1 Schur-complement conditioning step before picking #2 through #50—preventing redundant clustering.

Monitoring Gap Maps & Priority Station Placements

What Panels B & C Show:
  • Panel B (Variance Reduction Curve): The crimson curve tracks Cumulative % Variance Reduced as stations #1–#50 are added, pale blue bars show each site's marginal % gain, and the dashed teal curve traces Mean Kriging SE (Mean_SE_Before → Mean_SE_After)—revealing the diminishing-returns elbow where additional gauges begin to plateau.
  • Panel C (Top-12 Priority Reaches): A horizontal bar chart ranking candidates #1 to #12 with each reach's RivID, CatchID, Strahler stream order (O), local baseline Kriging SE, and individual % dV variance reduction for field reconnaissance.

Suggested Interpretation

  • 80.74% Evaluated Variance Drop from +50 Stations: Starting from 1,210 England RSN stations (mean Kriging SE 0.7961 log units), adding 50 optimally placed sites cuts Mean Kriging SE by -59.5% (0.7961 → 0.3228). Independent K = 50 basin designs cut variance by 32.31% in Humber, 43.55% in Thames, and 43.01% in Severn.
  • Schur-Complement De-Duplication: Updating v_eval and v_cand in place after every selection prevents picking neighbouring tributaries on the same river, dispersing Top-10 picks across Humber (#1 Riv 18175, +4.58% dV), Eden (#2 Riv 155540, +3.90% dV), Thames (#3 Riv 137890, +3.74% dV), Severn (#4 Riv 110339, +3.58% dV), and Tweed/Border (#5 Riv 149271, +3.50% dV).

Pairing --design-sites=50 with --threshold=uk lets teams prioritise gaps inside yellow Uncertain (20–80%) zones.

Case Study 7 · Multi-Approach Eutrophication Synthesis

Eutrophication Modelling: Driver–Response Co-Kriging, Non-Linear GAM & Seasonal DO

Evaluates three complementary ways to model river eutrophication across 90,876 England reaches using nutrient pressures (Orthophosphate 0180, Nitrate 0117), primary-producer response (Macrophyte Nutrient Index rmni, 1,687 samples across 1,098 reaches), and seasonal oxygen sag (Dissolved Oxygen 9901 % saturation, 20,915 samples). Compares cross-validated accuracy, spatial concordance, and practical trade-offs across all three paradigms.

Approach 1 · Driver–Response Co-Kriging

Best Basin Rescue

Methodology: Fits a 3-target Factor-LMC Joint NNGP (--method=lmc, K=3 latent factors, m=10 neighbors) coupling log-Orthophosphate (wq:0180, 1,210 reaches), log-Nitrate (wq:0117, 1,210 reaches), and macrophytes:rmni (1,098 reaches) on a 1,216-reach union support. Evaluates both All-Holdout (unmonitored reach) and Aux-Aided (nutrient-monitored, RMNI-unmonitored) CV.

Approach 2 · Non-Linear GAM + S3N

Best National RMNI R²

Methodology: Models macrophytes:rmni directly with REML-penalized cubic regression splines (--trend=gam) over catchment elevation, slope, upstream accumulation, and geology, then applies orthogonal Joint Additive NNGP (Tail-Up + Tail-Down + Euclidean) to spatial residuals. Benchmarked head-to-head against standard Linear S3N.

Approach 3 · Seasonal DO & P–RMNI

Symptom + Coupling

Methodology: Maps seasonal Dissolved Oxygen (9901 % saturation) via --by=season --st-borrow (precision-weighted temporal borrowing, ρt = 0.150) to isolate summer low-flow oxygen sag, and runs 04_compare_model_runs.R between 0180 and rmni to quantify network-wide 4-quadrant concordance.
